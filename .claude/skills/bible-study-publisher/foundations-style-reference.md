# Group Discussion Question Style Reference

Source: the Foundations program's breakout questions. Use these as the tone and style model for **Group Discussion Questions** — personally grounded, emotionally honest, inviting real sharing rather than performing theological knowledge.

1. Which of the four windows felt most alive to you? Which felt most distant or confusing?
2. The Armenian word Asdvadz means "the One who has put us here." Does that feel comforting, challenging, or somewhere in between?
3. We talked about arguments for God's existence — design, morality, causation. Do any resonate with your own experience? What's shaped your sense of whether God is real?

1. We used the fire analogy — source, light, and warmth. Have you encountered God more through "light" (seeing/understanding), "warmth" (feeling/being moved), or something else?
2. Each Person of the Trinity points to the others, not to Himself. What does that model of love look like in human relationships?
3. Through the Spirit, we're adopted as God's children — not employees, but family. Does that image change how you think about faith?

1. Have you ever felt a tension between your Armenian identity and your Christian faith? What did that look like?
2. The teaching says the Church's purpose is to lead people to God, not to preserve a culture. Do you agree? Does that change anything for you?

1. How does it land to be called a catechumen tonight — to sit in the learner's seat? And how does that change the way you think about church attendance or the value of your own mindset when you are in the Divine Liturgy?

1. Who in your life has been a "Church Father" to you — someone whose faith, words, or example helped hand the faith on to you? What gift did they give you that you still carry?
2. What's something the Church teaches that you've wrestled with — and what would it look like to hold it the way the Fathers did, with both deep loyalty and honest questioning?

1. Of the five models of prayer — liturgical, structured/prayer book, spontaneous, short repeated sentences, silence/stillness — which one sounds like where you are right now? And what's one small step you could take this week to try it? Don't overthink it. Pick the one that resonates. Share why. Then name one tiny step — not a big resolution, just a next thing.

**What makes these work:** they ask what the listener felt, experienced, or struggled with — never "what does the Church teach about X" in the abstract. They offer a concrete image or teaching from that session, then pivot immediately to the listener's own life. The last question in a session often asks for a small, concrete next step rather than a reflection.
