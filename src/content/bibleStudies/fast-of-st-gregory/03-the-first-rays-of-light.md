## Opening Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, who said, "Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you," write this word upon our hearts. As St. Gregory forgave the king who imprisoned him and baptized him in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, give us the courage to forgive those who have wounded us. Let our forgiveness be the first ray of light in someone else's darkness. Amen.

## Scripture Reading

**Matthew 5:44** — *"But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you."*

## Reflection

When Gregory finally emerges from thirteen years in the pit, his first act is not revenge — it is redemption. He seeks out the king who imprisoned him, and he baptizes him. He forgives him. This moment is more than personal reconciliation; it becomes the baptism of an entire nation. Armenia, once soaked in paganism, is reborn in Christ through an act of radical forgiveness. The Armenian Apostolic Church was not founded in a moment of triumph over enemies, but in the washing away of enmity itself. The devotional asks us to look at what we are holding onto — what pain, bitterness, or wound — that needs to be brought into the light and surrendered to grace. Our wounds, it suggests, may have a purpose beyond us.

## Group Discussion Questions

Gather with your group and discuss:

1. Gregory could have emerged from the pit with every justification for bitterness. Instead, he forgave and baptized the man who imprisoned him. What do you think makes that kind of forgiveness possible? Have you ever witnessed it — in someone else's life or your own?

2. The Church was born in radical forgiveness. Does that feel like a foundation you can stand on personally, or does it feel distant and difficult? What gets in the way?

3. The devotional says "maybe your pain has a purpose beyond you." Has a wound or hardship in your own life ever become something that helped or healed someone else?

## Personal Reflection Questions

Take some quiet time with these:

1. Is there a person or situation you need to forgive — something you have been carrying that needs to be released? What would a first step toward that look like?

2. Pray this prayer from the devotional: *"Lord, teach me to forgive as you forgave. Let my wounds be wells of healing. Shine your light through the cracks in my soul. Amen."* What crack in your soul most needs light right now?

## Closing Prayer

Holy Spirit, Fountain of light, deepen within us the forgiveness we have received. Where we still hold on to bitterness, loosen our grip. Where we have been wounded, make our wounds wells of healing. Send us out from this hour as bearers of Your light, that the world may see in us the first rays of the same dawn that rose over the baptized nation of Armenia. Amen.
